I am disappointed and sad and empty but not surprised. I am used to defending myself; silence calloused my insides.

What do you think I felt in my childhood? Was the silence between you and mother ever warm? There was a cold, cutting wind even in that deep southern state and I believed that that was love. I watched, in silence, trying to understand why the outside never matched what was inside. Outside, English, a language I learned on my own--the words like cheap stickers, slipping off the damp surfaces of things. Inside there was a wide silence there are still no words for.

I had my brother for awhile. I had grandma for less. Everything else was fear and silence.

I am curious and driven, not bad. I was never lost, only hidden, shoved into a corner like a secret when inconvenient. Mother taught me to fear my self, the same way she learned, to hide and hide and try. Mother was an orphan. You were too, in a different way. A mother or father's love is something neither of you knew--picked up in pieces from different people and things--a neighbor here, a friend there, maybe the Bible or a fireplace, both assembling from fragments a part of the whole, for your selves, because that's what you (we) needed to survive.

Some of the pieces you picked up made it to us, your sons. Gifts, mostly. What mother called care and what you hesitated to call love. I learned good things to, from you: how to treat strangers, how to be quiet and patient, how to handle problems--quietly, by myself, in secret.

There is no need to go over the past, again. I will tell you what you don't know.

My brother is hurt. I don't know if it was NY or mom or some combination of both. Or if it was you. He said you never talked to him, never had a real conversation. He thought you were dying when you asked to talk, he didn't believe me when I said you weren't dying. That was 2 years ago.

When he left NY and came to Seattle, he told me he was broke. He spent thousands of dollars on _____________. Years later, he told me he was ____________________________________. He kept that inside for over 4 years--almost as long as I was dead for. Remember what mother said when I told her I was assaulted in South Africa? She stared at me for a long time and said: "You weren't raped then..." Almost as if to say that it shouldn't be hard. What we heard was: you're wrong, you and your feelings are wrong.

Everytime I see him write with his right hand, I cannot make sense of it. We did not inherit the same superstitions, myths, or ignorance. We inherited the fear and loneliness and learned that there was right and wrong, not love, like the Bible says.

Maybe fear was your way of care. For a child walking by graveyards in the winter alone, maybe what you wanted was someone to share that with. You shared it, eventually, all of it, with all of us--through your hesitation and silence.

Maybe screaming was her way. Wanting to be heard by anyone as she waited year over year, end on end, even now, waiting for someone to save her. Grabbing and clinging--with white knuckles--onto everything and anything that was near. Forcing everything around her into a fence, creating an imaginary, beautiful cage.

I see my brother now inside the same cage, the one that mother built. When I went to court to sign the marriage papers he said: "I hope this is the last time." I could sense the sarcasm and disdain in his breath. He also said: "I've always respected you and never thought of you as less." That thought doesn't occur to a person that hasn't thought it. Children don't know fear, they learn it.

After the wedding, he went out with my then-wife while I was on a work trip. He joked about everyone looking at us wanting a threesome. He also echoed mother, calling me a slut, rewriting my history while I was away, telling her all the unnecessary imaginations he had while watching me from afar. He, like mother, spoke about me like a prop. Proud when there was something to be proud of, disdain for everything else. They need to compare, endlessly, to gauge where they are in everyone else's eyes except their own. Dragging people in to become parts of their cage.

I have decided to embrace my self and to do that, I need to get away from this. ______ saw it too. Every time I tried to bridge that wide silence with my brother, with mother, my head would spin and I would feel it again, that drowning feeling.

I don't need comparison or analogy. I am beginning to see myself as beautiful, even brilliant on some days. For the dark days and darker nights, I've never needed anyone other than my self--I thank you for that. It's the reason I can stand on my own, now.

I never felt safe to tell you about my life or decisions. Do you remember? In Europe, some roof of a building in some city you said: "it's not that big of a deal... in the big picture..." I remember feeling small, feeling myself shrink into that foreign air. I wanted to disappear. You were standing right next to me and I never felt so alone.

I'm tired of defending myself. I know that I was sincere, to the best of my ability, my whole life. I know that I ran away, often. I know that I hurt people. I know that. But how could I ever love what is gentle and kind when all I feel inside of me is storm?

I married with my eyes closed. It's just commitment. The storm will soften. It'll get better. That's what everyone said. She loved me, that's what she said. But I learned two broken things don't make a whole. They can make a child and silence and call it love, but that is not what it felt like at all--to me.

I went to Korea to be my self. I came to the first place I felt safe. When I first came here, to Jeju, 9 years ago, I remember wanting to stay. I came wanting to feel that again, the pull to stay. Wind, water, land, and all the hills. I don't know. It felt knowable, warm and light. So I came here and asked if you could would come. 26 hours altogether between planes, trains, airports. 36 years. I cried when I asked you for help because I knew. It was a dark night and you wouldn't be there. On the darkest nights, you were never there.

I am not my brother's keeper and I am not yours or mother's. I was a son to the people that treated me that way--with open arms not questions. I was a brother to anyone that needed a brother. I was asking to be your son but I no longer have to be. Some people don't change, orphans in particular are stubborn in their habits. I know because I've worked with them. I know because I feel like them. And I, like them, am terrified of my abandoned self.

For 36 years, mother thought my favorite food was the easiest thing for her to make. I never said anything after she threw away the teriyaki she made in Alabama, screaming at us to never eat again. Since then, I never cared for food. That's not what I needed to live on. My likes, hates, and everything deep, I hid in journals that mother would secretly read through. And then in photographs because at that age there was nowhere else to hide.

Maybe you don't trust poets or poems the way I do. A businessman said: "everything you want is on the other side of fear." I think Eliot said it better: "to lose beauty in terror, terror in inquisition." I wish I had a father that said this to me. I wish I had a mother who taught me bravery not fear. I wish my own family would sit with me in these questions instead of rushing to paint me one color or another. I wish I could love my self; I wish I could love someone wholly instead of in pieces.

I refuse to paint ______ in the same color she has painted me. We both tried. We were both broken and could only be patient for so long. We would've stayed together but it would be because of fear not love.

Convincing anyone is impossible. Believe what you want to believe--the Bible and God, rumor or myth, your words, hers--believe mine if you want.

Someday the sun will rise and everything--everything--will be well lit, even the shadows clearly defined. Until then, I will learn to embrace the wide silence carved into my chest. Sitting with people and questions like I used to. If the words are clumsy, it's because I learned on my own.
